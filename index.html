Before you start

AI can speed up the build. It can't build the business for you.

The book is honest about both sides of that sentence.

What's really true

  • AI can dramatically speed up tasks such as research, writing, design and simple website creation.
  • Zero-budget starts are possible using free-tier tools and personal time.
  • Small first wins are more realistic than overnight success.
  • First sales in documented cases often occurred around days 10–20.

What gets exaggerated

  • "AI built the whole thing" is misleading.
  • Humans still decide what to sell, who to sell to and how to price it.
  • Big revenue stories often involve experience, audiences, suppliers, capital or other existing advantages.
  • Building is easier than getting attention.
  • A finished product without distribution is not a business.

Chapter 3

Which model is right for you?

Three questions from the book that narrow the choice fast.

1. Do I need a sale today, or can I grow an audience over months?

If you need income quickly, pick a model with a short path to payment.

2. Am I better at making things, or at talking to people?

Match the model to your natural strength — don't fight it.

3. What can I actually finish in a spare few hours a day?

Choose something you can actually complete alongside everything else.

For someone with zero experience looking for a fast, lower-risk starting point, the book recommends a digital product or a single freelance service as a practical starting point.

Chapter 7

Getting your first sale

Five principles, then the practical checklist.

1

Sell to warm traffic first

Start with people who already know the problem — your network and relevant communities.

2

Give before you ask

Offer a free sample, lead magnet or honest help before asking for money.

3

Make the offer impossible to misunderstand

One clear sentence: what it is, who it's for, what they get.

4

Show up daily, even briefly

Consistency beats intensity. A little progress every day compounds.

5

Ask for the sale directly

Make the ask explicit — no one buys what they weren't asked to buy.

Your first-sale checklist

  • One-sentence pitch
  • Working payment method
  • Free lead magnet or sample
  • List of 20 real people or communities
  • Direct ask to at least 5 people a day
Chapter 8

Avoid these 6 mistakes

The exact mistakes the book documents — before they cost you a month.

1

Building before validating

Making the product before checking anyone wants it.

2

Tool-hopping instead of shipping

Endlessly switching tools instead of finishing and publishing.

3

Publishing generic AI output unedited

Posting raw AI copy without your own voice and a human check.

4

Confusing "built" with "launched"

A finished product without distribution is not a business.

5

Picking a niche that's too broad

Everyone means no one — narrow audiences convert better.

6

Giving up after one quiet week

Attention is slower than building. One quiet week is normal.

  • Raise your price or build a second offer
If you got interest but few/no sales

Run a second, faster loop

  • Talk to people who almost bought
  • Find the real objection
  • Narrow the audience
  • Run a second, faster loop
If you're ready to grow

Grow with proof

  • Reinvest once revenue is consistent
  • Consider better tools
  • Consider paid promotion

  • Don't burn cash before proving the offer
